# Geopolitical Risk & Market Liquidity: Analyzing the 2026 U.S - Iran Conflict

# Overview
This project investigates market behavior during the 2026 U.S.–Iran conflict. By analyzing how different financial assets behaved during a period of geopolitical uncertainty. The goal is to understand how capital flows between asset classes under stress and how markets react to disruption

The focus is on:
- Returns (daily performance)
- Drawdowns (crash severity)
- Volatility (risk)
- Recovery patterns
- Comparative asset behavior

# Approach
The project follows a hybrid workflow:

**SQL Layer:**
- Designed views using window functions to calculate returns, rolling volatility, and drawdowns

**Python Layer:**
- Used for visualization and deeper analysis, including trend comparison and market behavior insights

# Key Insights
- Crude oil reacted quickly as tensions escalated, with prices rising sharply as concerns about supply disruptions grew, especially around the Strait of Hormuz. Even after the ceasefire, prices stayed elevated instead of returning to earlier levels, pointing to ongoing uncertainty.

- The S&P 500 moved differently from oil during this period. While oil was surging, the index remained relatively stable, likely because strength in some sectors helped offset weakness in others.

- The sharp rise in oil prices seems to have created pressure on liquidity, as higher energy costs forced investors to free up cash. This likely contributed to selling in gold, despite its usual role as a safe-haven asset.

- Overall, the pattern suggests that during periods of stress, capital doesn’t just move toward “safe” assets—it often shifts toward liquidity, with investors prioritizing flexibility over traditional safety.
